# PronounceLab Student Experience Manifesto

> The canonical philosophy for every learner-facing product decision.

PronounceLab exists to help people become more confident speaking English. This document describes what learning inside PronounceLab should feel like. It is the standard against which learner experiences are imagined, designed, written, reviewed, and improved.

## 1. The Vision

Learning English should feel possible today—not someday after years of study, and not only when a learner feels ready to be judged.

Pronunciation is more than producing the right sounds. It is the confidence to join a conversation, ask a question, introduce yourself, speak at work, and be understood without retreating into silence. When speech becomes clearer, participation becomes easier. When participation becomes easier, English becomes part of everyday life.

PronounceLab exists to make that change approachable. It turns expert teaching into short, purposeful experiences that help learners understand, notice, practise, speak, and continue. It respects the courage required to use a new language aloud. It gives learners a place where repetition is normal, mistakes are useful, and improvement is visible.

The experience should never make fluency feel distant or mysterious. It should make the next useful step feel clear.

## 2. Our Philosophy

### Learning comes before gamification

Every feature must serve practice, understanding, recall, or confident speech. Rewards may strengthen a learning habit, but they must never become the reason the lesson exists.

### Confidence comes before perfection

Clear communication matters more than imitation of an ideal accent. We help learners improve without suggesting that their voice is a defect.

### Small progress is real progress

One sound understood, one contrast heard, or one sentence spoken more clearly is meaningful. The experience should recognize specific effort without exaggerating it.

### Consistency beats intensity

Short, repeatable practice creates familiarity. PronounceLab should make returning easy, even after a long absence, and should never punish a learner for the life that happened between lessons.

### There is always a next step

Learners should not have to interpret the product before they can learn. At each moment, one useful action should be clear. Choice remains available, but direction comes first.

### Familiarity reduces fear

Repeated patterns, stable language, and predictable interactions conserve attention for learning. English becomes less intimidating when the experience itself feels dependable.

### Practice is a safe place to be unfinished

Errors are information, not identity. Feedback should guide the next attempt and preserve the learner’s willingness to speak again.

### Simplicity protects attention

Every unnecessary choice competes with learning. We remove noise, explain unfamiliar actions, and reveal complexity only when it becomes useful.

### Honest encouragement builds trust

We celebrate what the learner actually did. We do not invent progress, overstate accuracy, or use praise that feels automatic. Truthful encouragement is more motivating than empty applause.

### Technology should recede

The learner should remember the sound, phrase, idea, and moment of progress—not the machinery that delivered them. Technology earns its place by making good teaching easier to experience.

### Every lesson should end with capability

A learner should leave knowing something, hearing something, or saying something they could not do as confidently before. Completion is not merely reaching the last screen; it is feeling ready for the next attempt.

## 3. Student Emotions

The emotional journey matters as much as the route through the product.

### Home

The learner should feel oriented and welcomed back. Home says, “Here is the most useful thing you can do now.” It should create momentum without pressure and make returning after interruption feel normal.

### Courses

The learner should feel possibility without overload. Courses are understandable learning journeys, not a warehouse of content. The learner should be able to choose with confidence and understand where each journey may take them.

### Units

The learner should feel structure and forward motion. Units turn a large goal into a sequence that feels manageable. Completed work should provide reassurance; the next step should remain easy to recognize.

### Lessons

The learner should feel focused, safe, and active. A lesson asks for attention one meaningful task at a time. Instructions are clear, practice is generous, and feedback helps rather than interrupts.

### Lesson Complete

The learner should feel genuine accomplishment and readiness. The moment should acknowledge the work just done, name progress honestly, and offer a calm next choice. It should never feel like an arcade reward detached from learning.

### Future Progress

The learner should feel informed, not measured. Progress should reveal growth, patterns, and useful next steps. It must never turn incomplete work into shame or reduce language learning to a collection of numbers.

### Future Achievements

The learner should feel recognized for meaningful learning behavior. Achievements should tell a true story—consistent practice, a completed journey, a difficult contrast revisited—not distribute decoration for trivial actions.

### Future Assignments

The learner should feel supported and prepared, not monitored. An assignment should make its purpose, expectations, and next action clear while preserving the sense that learning belongs to the student.

## 4. Language Guidelines

PronounceLab speaks like a calm, capable teacher: direct, respectful, warm, and specific.

Prefer words that connect to learning and movement:

- **Mission** for a purposeful speaking challenge.
- **Journey** for a meaningful sequence of learning.
- **Practice** for repeated work without judgment.
- **Improve** for growth that remains possible.
- **Continue** for returning to work already begun.
- **Review** for strengthening completed learning.
- **Complete** for finishing a meaningful step.
- **Progress** for demonstrated movement over time.
- **Learn** for understanding before performance.
- **Encouragement** that names real effort or improvement.

Avoid learner-facing words such as **Dashboard**, **Statistics**, **Administration**, **Management**, and **Reports**. They describe systems, oversight, and measurement rather than learning. Use **Home**, **Progress**, **Courses**, or a specific action instead.

Use verbs that explain what happens next. Prefer “Continue lesson” to “Proceed,” “Review this sound” to “Access content,” and “Try again” to language that implies failure. Avoid institutional vocabulary, unexplained technical terms, false urgency, and praise unsupported by the learner’s actions.

Write for adults without becoming formal or cold. Be concise without becoming abrupt. Never speak to learners as if they are children, problems to be fixed, or data points to be optimized.

## 5. Visual Philosophy

The visual experience should make attention feel guided, not captured.

**Hierarchy** makes the learning purpose and next action unmistakable. One primary idea leads each moment; supporting information stays visibly secondary.

**Whitespace** creates calm and gives language room to be read, heard, and considered. Density is justified only when comparison is itself the learning task.

**Color** communicates structure, action, feedback, and status with restraint. It supports meaning but never carries meaning alone. Celebration may become warmer or brighter, but it should not overwhelm the learner’s accomplishment.

**Typography** prioritizes effortless reading. Headings orient; body text teaches; labels clarify. Comfortable line lengths, reliable contrast, and generous spacing matter more than decorative personality.

**Motion** explains change: where something came from, what became active, or what was completed. It is brief, optional where necessary, and never delays practice or demands attention for its own sake.

**Cards** group one coherent idea or action. They should not turn every sentence into a container or fragment a lesson into visual noise.

**Buttons** describe outcomes clearly and reflect a truthful hierarchy. The primary button advances learning; secondary buttons support choice; destructive actions are rare, explicit, and recoverable where possible.

**Illustrations** should reduce anxiety, clarify an idea, or create a moment of warmth. They must respect adult learners and never substitute decoration for explanation.

**Icons** reinforce familiar meaning. They remain consistent, understandable, and paired with words whenever the action may be ambiguous.

## 6. Interaction Philosophy

Learning interactions should feel fast, predictable, calm, and forgiving.

The same action should behave the same way wherever it appears. A learner should see immediate, proportionate feedback and never wonder whether an answer, recording, or completion was accepted. Waiting should be explained. Errors should preserve context and offer a useful recovery path.

The experience should never create surprise through hidden consequences. It should not interrupt practice with unnecessary prompts, compete for attention with multiple primary actions, or use urgency to manufacture engagement.

Learners must be free to pause, repeat, review, and return. Repetition is a core learning behavior, not a sign that something went wrong. Progress should survive ordinary interruption wherever truthfully possible, and stale information should never trap the learner.

Encouragement belongs at moments of effort, understanding, recovery, and completion. It should help the learner take the next step rather than stop to admire the interface.

## 7. Motivation

Motivation in PronounceLab means helping learners believe that practice is worthwhile and that returning is possible.

The strongest motivation comes from understanding more, speaking more clearly, recognizing improvement, and knowing what to do next. Product mechanics should reinforce those experiences rather than compete with them.

XP, badges, streaks, and achievements are not learning outcomes. If introduced, they must represent real and clearly explained behavior. They must not reward empty repetition, punish missed days, exploit anxiety, or imply precision the product cannot support. A streak should invite return, not create fear of loss. A badge should preserve a meaningful memory, not fill an empty collection. XP should never become more important than practice.

No motivational system should be added until its learning purpose, rules, accessibility, truthfulness, and likely emotional effect are understood.

## 8. Accessibility

Accessibility is part of teaching well.

A learner who cannot hear an example clearly, operate a control, read an instruction, distinguish a state, or recover from an error is not merely encountering an interface problem; they are being excluded from the lesson.

PronounceLab must support different ways of seeing, hearing, moving, reading, processing, remembering, and concentrating. Content should remain understandable without color alone. Audio needs meaningful alternatives where the lesson allows them. Controls need clear names, visible focus, comfortable targets, and logical order. Motion must respect learner preferences. Language must reduce cognitive burden without reducing adult dignity.

Accessibility should be present in the first design conversation, not added after the experience is considered complete. The goal is not only compliance. The goal is confidence, independence, and equal access to practice.

## 9. Future Principles

New learner features should earn their place. Before implementation, ask:

1. Does this help students become more confident speaking English?
2. Does it improve understanding, practice, recall, or communication?
3. Does it reduce friction or add a new burden?
4. Does it encourage sustainable consistency without pressure or shame?
5. Is the feedback honest about what the product knows?
6. Is the next useful action clearer because this exists?
7. Does it respect adult learners and different abilities?
8. Can a learner recover easily from interruption, error, or absence?
9. Does the technology support the teaching—or call attention to itself?
10. Would we still want this feature five years from now?

If a feature cannot answer these questions well, it should be changed, deferred, or removed. Novelty is not a reason to compromise trust. Engagement is not a reason to increase anxiety. More data is not automatically more insight. More choice is not automatically more freedom.

## 10. The PronounceLab Promise

We help people improve their English every day.

We make the next step clear.

We teach before we test, and encourage before we judge.

We treat mistakes as part of learning and every learner’s voice with dignity.

We value consistent practice over intensity, confidence over perfection, and real progress over invented rewards.

We build experiences that are calm, accessible, honest, and worth returning to.

We use technology to bring good teaching closer—and then let it disappear.

Every lesson should leave the learner more familiar with English, more willing to speak, and more certain that improvement is possible.

**Improve your English every day.**
